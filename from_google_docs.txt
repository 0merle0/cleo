
Abstract

Recent tools for de novo design have revolutionized the way we propose novel proteins and the types of challenges we are able to tackle. While these tools have dramatically increased the hit rate of functional designs, zero-shot generations often require further optimization to enhance the desired properties. We present a novel method for generating high fidelity diverse combinatorial libraries. Traditional library generation methods rely on error prone PCR or natural fragment shuffling to generate diversity. Such approaches do not take into account the filtering of designs through structure prediction models or other desired oracles. We demonstrate the ability to post-train inverse folding models to design fragments with filter pass rates of XX% and libraries which can easily scale beyond 1012 individually addressable variants. Our post-training framework enables control over the search space by centering the library around a reference sequence with tunable mutation distance. Once designed, we demonstrate how individual variants are assembled with simple molecular biology techniques (golden gate assembly). Our cell-free assay platform enables us to screen enzymes at the scale of 103 measurements per day, each with replicates. The libraries generated are easily much larger than most traditional and modern high throughput assays are able to screen, so we demonstrate how the library can be sub sampled smartly to optimize enzymes. For a de novo designed PETase we demonstrate our platform finds a variant with 250x increase in catalytic activity.
