\section{Results: fragment recombination builds the library}
\label{sec:res:fragments}

\plan{\textbf{Claim.} Designs from the trained policy can be cut at fixed
boundaries and recombined across parents, so that $\sum_i |F_i|$ synthesized DNA
parts yield $\prod_i |F_i|$ testable variants --- and the recombinants keep
enough of the parental pass rate that the resulting library is worth building.

\textbf{Status: \statusscaffold{}.} The computational half is measured; the
section is still written as a plan because the blocks below are what the prose
has to become. The \emph{physical} half is no longer hypothetical:
Section~\ref{sec:res:experimental} reports a five-fragment library built by
exactly this procedure --- 128 protein parts spanning $1.1 \times 10^{7}$
recombinants, 3{,}135 of them assembled and assayed, 67\% registering above
background at a median of 29 mutations from the parent. That is the one
experimental test in this paper of the independence assumption named below.

\textbf{Why it is the load-bearing section.} Section~\ref{sec:res:frontier}
buys a better \emph{distribution}; on its own that is a sampling result. This
section is what converts a distribution into something you can physically order:
a library assembled from $k$ fragment sets is the product of their sizes, so
recombination is what buys library scale that sampling alone cannot. Without it
the paper is a benchmark result.}

\subsection*{Block 1 --- The combinatorial argument, stated precisely}

\begin{planlist}
  \item Set up the notation: $L$ residues split into $k$ fragments at fixed
        boundaries $[s_i, e_i]$; $F_i$ the set of distinct sequences observed at
        fragment $i$ across the policy's output; a recombinant is one choice per
        slot, joined.
  \item The arithmetic: $\sum_i |F_i|$ parts, $\prod_i |F_i|$ recombinants. With
        $k = 5$ and $|F_i| = 40$ that is 200 parts and $1.0 \times 10^8$
        variants. \textbf{State the actual numbers used, not this example.}
  \item Golden Gate assembly is what makes the product physically reachable:
        one pot, orthogonal overhangs at the boundaries, no per-variant
        synthesis. Methods carries the adapter design.
  \item \textbf{The assumption the whole section rests on, named up front:}
        fragments are treated as if they contribute independently to
        foldability. They do not, exactly --- the whole point of an inverse
        folding model is that residues are coupled. Block 3 measures how badly
        the approximation fails, and that measurement is the result.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{Block 3 --- Do recombinants fold? (the experiment that must be run)}

\plan{\textbf{This is the measurement the section exists for.} Everything above
is setup and everything below is consequence. Design, in the order it should be
run:

\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item Sample $n$ designs from a trained \cleo{} policy with no selection
        applied, on an AME backbone where the parental pass rate is already
        known and high (M0097 is the obvious choice --- it is the backbone every
        other measurement in the paper uses).
  \item Split each at the fragment boundaries. Keep only fragments drawn from
        \emph{passing} parents, since that is what one would actually order.
  \item Draw recombinants uniformly from the product space, excluding any
        recombinant that reproduces a parent exactly.
  \item Fold best-of-5 and score on the identical code path as
        Section~\ref{sec:res:frontier}. \textbf{Same pipeline, same reference,
        or the comparison to the parental rate is not a comparison.}
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{The endpoint is retention:} recombinant pass rate divided by parental
pass rate. Everything else is secondary.

\textbf{Pre-registered predictions, written before the run:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item Retention is well above the rate for recombining across \emph{random}
        MPNN samples at matched diversity. If it is not, fragmentation is
        adding nothing that temperature would not.
  \item Retention falls monotonically with the number of slots swapped away
        from a single parent. A recombinant differing at one slot should behave
        nearly like its parent; one differing at all $k$ should be worst. A flat
        curve would mean the fragments are effectively independent, which would
        be a stronger result than we expect and should be reported as such.
  \item Recombinants reach mutational combinations absent from the parental set
        --- measured as $U$ over the recombinant library against $U$ over the
        parents at matched design count.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What kills the claim:} retention below the point where the library
is filterable in practice. If the parental rate is \SI{57}{\percent} and
recombinants pass at \SI{2}{\percent}, the product space is nominal rather than
real, and the honest paper reports the fragment library as a screening funnel
with a stated hit rate instead of as a $10^8$-member library. Decide the
threshold now, not after seeing the number.}

\textbf{This experiment has now been run.} The rest of this block reports it.
The pre-registration above is left in place so the predictions can be read
against the outcome rather than reconstructed after it.

Three AME backbones, 256 designs sampled per arm with no selection applied,
split at uniform boundaries into $k=4$ slots, recombined uniformly over the
product space with exact parents excluded, and folded best-of-5 through
\texttt{af3\_from\_df} --- the identical evaluator used for the parental rate
and for every library number in this paper, so the comparison is a comparison.

\textbf{Recombinants fold, and training for recombination is what makes them
fold.} Under the chimera objective at matched batch the recombinant pass rate
is \emph{above} the parental rate on M0097 (85.5\% against 84.4\%) and on
M0255 (97.3\% against 94.9\%), and 1.5 points below it on M0315 (97.3\%
against 98.8\%, where the parents are nearly saturated). Cutting a passing
design into four parts and reassembling it across parents costs at most a
point and a half of foldability, and on two of three backbones costs nothing
at all --- so the product space is real rather than nominal.

\textbf{The comparison that carries weight is against the seed band, not
against a single baseline.} The two plain-RL rows per backbone are the same
configuration run twice, and they bound what a lucky draw can buy. At matched
batch the chimera arm clears the full observed band on every backbone: +14.4
points on M0097 (85.5\% against 68.0--71.1\%), +66.8 points on M0255 (97.3\%
against 29.3--30.5\%), and +32.1 points on M0315 (97.3\% against
57.0--65.2\%). Measured against each backbone's own seed-to-seed spread in the
recombinant rate (3.1, 1.2 and 8.2 points) those margins are 4.6$\times$,
56$\times$ and 3.9$\times$.

That is the honest version of a claim earlier drafts made more strongly. The
margins quoted previously (+25.8, +52.0, +34.8) came from the $B=32$ chimera
rows, which differ from the plain-RL arms in batch size and sample reuse as
well as in objective and oracle. Batch-matching costs M0097 eleven points of
margin and gains M0255 fifteen. One caveat survives the correction and should
be stated rather than buried: M0097's +14.4 is \emph{below} the 21-point
control-pass swing documented between two unseeded runs of a single
configuration elsewhere in this work, so on that backbone the margin alone does
not clear the widest variance we have measured. M0255 and M0315 clear it
comfortably.

\textbf{Retention is the least reproducible statistic in this table and should
not be read as a point estimate.} It is a ratio whose numerator is stable and
whose denominator is not. Across the plain-RL replicates the recombinant rate
moves by 1.2--8.2 points between seeds while the \emph{parental} rate moves by
3.1--44.5 points, so retention swings from 0.767 to 1.611 on M0097 for a
configuration that was not changed at all. It has therefore been dropped as a
column: the recombinant pass rate is the endpoint, and it is what the table
reports.

Retention is also not the same quantity across arms, which is the second reason
to stop tabulating it. The recombination step filters parents to those that
already pass, so for an arm whose parents are drawn from a pre-filtered pool the
denominator is 1.0 by construction, while for the arms here it is the pass rate
of the 256 \emph{sampled} designs. Dividing the unselected-MPNN control by the
same rule gives retention of 1.53, 7.59 and 1.43 --- values that are obviously
not foldability ratios, and that show the column was never comparing like with
like. This is a general caution about retention as an
endpoint, and it revises the framing of the pre-registration above, which named
retention as the endpoint and everything else as secondary.

\textbf{Scoring the pre-registered predictions.} The first holds: recombining
across unselected MPNN samples gives a library pass rate of \SI{45.1}{\percent}
against the chimera arm's \SI{93.1}{\percent} pooled over backbones. \textbf{The third was
ill-posed, and scoring it properly is more informative than the prediction
would have been.} It asked whether the recombinant library carries more
distinct substitutions than its parents at matched design count. It cannot.
Every recombinant is a concatenation of parent fragments, so every
(position, residue) pair it carries appears in some parent \emph{at that
position}: the recombinant repertoire is a subset of the parental one by
construction, and no training objective can change that. Earlier drafts
reported the prediction as holding, on a pair of numbers (1{,}409 against
1{,}139) that no script on disk produces and that were rarefied at different
depths.

Measured as the ratio it actually is, recombination is a mild subset. At
matched design count the recombinant library retains \SI{87}{\percent} of the
parental repertoire at $k=4$ (0.876, 0.848, 0.880 on M0097, M0255, M0315) and
\SI{92}{\percent} at $k=16$ (0.944, 0.913, 0.913), while multiplying the number
of variants carrying it by four to thirty orders of magnitude depending on $k$.
That $k=16$ loses less than $k=4$, with no overlap between the two ranges, is
the expected direction: shorter fragments mean each slot's substitutions recur
across more recombinants, so fewer are lost to any particular draw.

\textbf{The objective does not make the subset gentler --- it makes the set
being subsetted larger.} The preservation ratio is not what the chimera
objective improves. Recombining unselected LigandMPNN draws preserves
\SI{93}{\percent}, marginally the highest of any arm, because a smaller and
more redundant parental repertoire has less unique material to lose: that arm
retains 836, 584 and 1{,}222 substitutions out of 854, 668 and 1{,}294. The
chimera arm at $k=16$ retains 1{,}095, 1{,}187 and 1{,}320 out of 1{,}160,
1{,}300 and 1{,}445 --- a slightly smaller fraction of a substantially larger
repertoire, and in absolute terms between \num{1.1} and \num{2.0} times as many
surviving substitutions, at three times the library pass rate. A ratio close to
one is cheap to achieve by having little to lose, which is why the absolute
count is the quantity that matters here. \textbf{The second is not yet
measured.} The retention-versus-slots-swapped curve scores recombinants by
their Hamming distance in slot space from the nearest parent. That distance is
\emph{computable from the scored libraries already on disk} --- earlier drafts
recorded it as a missing pipeline feature, which was wrong. The real obstacle
is the sampling: uniform recombination over the product space at $k=4$ puts
essentially every draw at three slots swapped (249 of 256 on M0097), so the
axis has no support at $d=1,2,4$ and the curve cannot be fitted. Measuring it
needs a stratified draw at fixed $d$, not a new metric. It remains the one
pre-registered prediction this experiment does not address, but it is cheap
rather than blocked.

\textbf{The chimera objective buys pass rate by narrowing the policy, and
cutting finer buys it back.} Pointing the library table at the chimera arms
makes a cost visible that a comparison against an obsolete arm had hidden.
Against plain RL the chimera arms carry fewer distinct substitutions per
library and sit closer together in sequence space, at a markedly higher pass
rate:

Three things follow. First, the cost is \emph{within} RL: every chimera arm
carries more substitutions than the no-RL control (1{,}154 and 1{,}201 against
881) at triple its pass rate, so this is a statement about the objective and
not about whether function-aligned search moves the frontier. Second, the cost
is concentrated in one cell --- M0255 at $k=4$ falls to Hamming 42.4 against
plain RL's 73.2, while M0097 and M0315 sit at 68.6 and 78.0 against 72.0 and
84.4 --- and M0255 is also the backbone where the fragment-count sweep is
non-monotone, so the two may be one instability. Third, \textbf{$k=16$
dominates $k=4$ on both axes}: more substitutions (950 against 895), greater
spread (71.2 against 63.0), the same pass rate, and a product space larger by
twenty-five orders of magnitude. The diversity given up by cutting into four
parts is largely recovered by cutting into sixteen.

\subsection*{Block 4 --- Relation to manufacturing-aware generative models}

\plan{\citet{weinstein2026manufacturing} is the nearest prior art and the
section must engage it directly rather than in passing. Their move is to make
the generative model manufacturing-aware from the outset --- factorized to match
what combinatorial DNA chemistry can physically sample --- and they reach
${\sim}10^{16}$ designs for antibodies, T-cell antigens and Taq polymerase.

\textbf{The honest framing.} They get there first and at larger scale. What is
different is the regime, and it is a real difference rather than a hedge:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item \textbf{Their objective comes from a natural distribution.} 300 million
        observed human antibodies define what a good sample looks like, so the
        model can be constrained to the chemistry from the start without giving
        anything up. For a de novo enzyme there is no such distribution --- the
        backbone has no natural homologs --- so the objective has to be
        constructed, which is what Section~\ref{sec:res:frontier} does.
  \item \textbf{Order of operations.} They factorize first and train the
        factorized model; we train an unfactorized policy for catalytic geometry
        and factorize afterwards. That makes retention (Block 3) our problem and
        not theirs --- their samples are manufacturable by construction, ours
        have to be shown to survive the cut.
  \item \textbf{Scale is not the axis we win on and the section should say so
        plainly.} $10^{16}$ is out of reach here and it does not need to be
        reached: a $10^{8}$ library that is enriched for catalytic geometry is a
        different object from a $10^{16}$ library that is not.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Do not write this block as a competitive comparison.} The two results
compose --- a manufacturing-aware architecture trained against a catalytic
reward is the obvious next thing, and saying so is more useful than claiming a
win.}
