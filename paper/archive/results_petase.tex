\section{Results: three rounds of closed-loop optimization on a de novo PETase}
\label{sec:res:petase}

\plan{\textbf{Claim.} Three successive libraries, each designed using the
measured activity of the previous one, take a de novo PETase from a weak
starting design to \textbf{[250]}$\times$ faster --- above every characterized
natural enzyme on the same probe.

\textbf{Status: \statusblocked{}.} The assays exist; the numbers are not yet in
this repository. Each block below names exactly what file or measurement it
needs, so the section can be filled in without re-deriving the structure.

\textbf{Why three rounds and not one big library.} The escalation is the
argument. Round 1 uses an easy fluorogenic probe because a cold-start library
needs a permissive assay to produce any signal at all. Round 2 moves to a
substrate closer to real PET \emph{and} gets larger, which is only affordable
because of Section~\ref{sec:res:fragments}. Round 3 is the closed loop proper:
the design objective now contains a surrogate trained on rounds 1 and 2. Each
round changes exactly one thing, which is what makes the final number
attributable.}

\subsection*{Block 1 --- Starting point and the assay ladder}
\begin{planlist}
  \item The de novo PETase backbone: origin, catalytic triad, why it is a
        genuine de novo design and not a remodelled natural PETase.
  \item Baseline activity of the starting design --- the denominator for every
        fold-improvement number in this section. \textbf{State it once, here,
        with units, and refer back to it.}
  \item The three probes in order of stringency: the easy fluorogenic probe,
        the PET-proximal probe, and bulk PET. Say up front which claims are on
        which probe --- the single most likely way this section misleads a
        referee is by quoting a fold-improvement from one probe next to a
        comparison from another.
  \item Comparator panel: which natural and engineered PETases were assayed
        \emph{in-house on the same probe}. Literature values are not a
        comparison and should not be presented as one.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{Block 2 --- Round 1: pilot library on the easy probe}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Library 1: designed by function-aligned RL sampling, no surrogate ---
        the reward is computational only.
  \item Size, fragment structure, mutation-distance distribution from the
        starting design.
  \item Activity distribution: hit rate, best variant, fold over start.
  \item \textbf{What round 1 has to establish:} that the library contains
        signal at all, and that the signal spans enough range to train on. A
        library where everything is dead teaches the surrogate nothing.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{Block 3 --- Round 2: larger library, harder probe}
\begin{planlist}
  \item What changed from round 1, and what deliberately did not.
  \item Library 2 size --- this is where the fragment combinatorics pay off, so
        the number should be stated against the count of DNA parts actually
        ordered.
  \item Activity on the PET-proximal probe: hit rate, distribution, best
        variant.
  \item \textbf{Expect the hit rate to fall} moving to the harder probe. Report
        it falling. A round that got harder and easier at the same time invites
        the reader to wonder what else changed.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{Block 4 --- Round 3: the surrogate closes the loop}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Surrogate: MLP ensemble on (sequence, activity) pairs pooled from
        rounds 1 and 2. Architecture, training split, held-out calibration.
  \item \textbf{Report held-out predictive performance before reporting what it
        designed.} A surrogate that cannot rank held-out variants did not cause
        the improvement, whatever the improvement turns out to be.
  \item Surrogate added as a reward step in the RL pipeline; library 3
        composition and mutation distance.
  \item Activity distribution of library 3 against library 2 on the same probe
        --- \textbf{the whole-distribution comparison, not just the best
        variant.} A shifted distribution is evidence the loop closed; a single
        outlier is evidence of a bigger library.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{Block 5 --- The best construct}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Full kinetics on the top variant: $k_\mathrm{cat}$, $K_\mathrm{M}$,
        $k_\mathrm{cat}/K_\mathrm{M}$, against the starting design under
        identical conditions.
  \item The \textbf{[250]}$\times$ figure: state precisely which quantity, which
        probe, which comparator.
  \item Head-to-head against the natural and engineered comparators on that
        probe.
  \item Mutation distance from the starting design, and how far outside SSM
        range that is --- this closes the loop back to
        Section~\ref{sec:intro}'s framing.
  \item Structural rationalization if a structure exists; clearly marked as
        post-hoc if so.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{Block 6 --- What this does not show}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Bulk PET. If activity on solid substrate does not follow the probe
        results, say so in the main text rather than the supplement, and
        attribute it to substrate accessibility only if there is evidence for
        that attribution.
  \item Cold start. The loop needs a first library with measurable spread; this
        section does not show how to get one on a system where round 1 comes
        back flat.
  \item One backbone, one chemistry. The generality claim rests on
        Section~\ref{sec:res:frontier}, which is 41 sites, not on this section.
\end{planlist}
