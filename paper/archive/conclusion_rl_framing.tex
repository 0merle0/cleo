\subsection*{P-A --- The central takeaway}
\begin{planlist}
  \item The active-variant region for a de novo enzyme is farther from the
        starting design than SSM can reach and more specific than temperature
        sampling can hit. Both halves of that sentence are measured in this
        paper, and neither is measured by prior work.
  \item The resolution is not a better sampler or a bigger library on its own.
        It is a sampler trained against function, factorized into parts that can
        be physically assembled, and closed against measured activity. Each
        piece is necessary; state why.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{P-B --- What each move established}
\begin{planlist}
  \item \textbf{Frontier.} A KL-anchored RL policy occupies a region of the
        diversity/fidelity plane that no temperature reaches. The anchor is the
        mechanism, not a detail --- unanchored, the policy collapses onto a
        narrow solution with worse per-design coverage than the baseline.
  \item \textbf{Fragments.} Recombination converts that distribution into a
        library exponentially larger than the parts ordered, at a measured cost
        in pass rate.
  \item \textbf{PETase.} Three rounds, each designed on the last round's data,
        ending above characterized natural enzymes on the same probe.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{P-C --- Honest limitations}
\begin{planlist}
  \item \textbf{Bulk PET.} Probe activity is not plastic degradation. Do not let
        this paragraph soften.
  \item \textbf{Run-to-run variance.} Single-run comparisons of pass rate on
        this benchmark are not interpretable at the effect sizes usually of
        interest; two draws of one config moved 21 points. Every claim retained
        in this paper either clears that width or is replicated. \emph{Cite the
        notebook's E17/E19/E21 record for the measurement.}
  \item \textbf{The reward and the evaluation share a predictor.} Motif RMSD
        under AF3 appears on both sides of the frontier result. A held-out
        oracle remains the outstanding control, and the paper should say so
        rather than wait to be asked.
  \item \textbf{Selection during training does not help.} Five experiments
        looked for a rule that beats random selection of rollouts and none
        found one. Worth one sentence as a negative result others need not
        repeat.
  \item \textbf{Cold start.} The loop needs a first library with measurable
        spread.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{P-D --- Outlook}
\begin{planlist}
  \item The obvious composition: a manufacturing-aware architecture
        \citep{weinstein2026manufacturing} trained against a catalytic reward,
        rather than factorizing after the fact as we do here. That gets the
        scale of the former and the objective of the latter, and it is the next
        experiment rather than a distant prospect.
  \item More rounds, and rewards that mix computational and measured signal
        continuously rather than once per library.
  \item Other chemistries and substrate classes, including bulk polymers.
\end{planlist}
