\subsection*{P-A --- Central scientific takeaway}
\begin{planlist}
  \item The active-variant region for a de novo enzyme is broader and farther
        from the starting design than SSM or vanilla MPNN can reach.
  \item Function-aligned RL sampling + combinatorial library construction +
        experimental closed loop together close the gap.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{P-B --- What each application individually established}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Heme: fine-tuned MPNN dominates SSM and vanilla MPNN under matched
        experimental effort --- the foundation.
  \item PETase: experimental data $\rightarrow$ surrogate reward $\rightarrow$
        improved next library; a closed loop, not a single shot.
  \item Protease: explicit diversity pressure (batch-consensus divergence
        reward) unlocks rare mutations and improves activity over an
        otherwise-matched library.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{P-C --- Honest limitations}
\begin{planlist}
  \item PETase: probe and dimer activity demonstrated; bulk PET activity
        remains an open challenge attributed to substrate accessibility on
        solid plastic.
  \item Closed-loop iteration depends on having an assay-ready experimental
        pipeline; cold-start is still hard.
  \item Diversity reward improves access to rare mutations but does not
        substitute for a well-specified function reward.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{P-D --- Outlook}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Generalization to additional chemistries and substrate classes,
        including bulk polymers.
  \item Tighter integration of design-time rewards with experimental surrogate
        signals across more rounds.
\end{planlist}
