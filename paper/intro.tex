\section{Introduction}

As the age of de novo design begins to mature, we have access to a multitude of
tools which allow us to design enzymes. The sequence to function landscape
surrounding a given seed sequence is not well understood. There are clear
pathways and routes of evolution that exist to evolved sequences along a
specified trajectory. Inverse folding models allow us to sample from the space
of possible sequences for a given
backbone~\cite{dauparas2022proteinmpnn,dauparas2025ligandmpnn,hsu2022esmif}.
This is useful as small changes in sequence space can amount to orders of
magnitude change in function. For a given function we struggle to understand
why certain sequences for a given backbone are functional while others are not.
Filtering by looking at the geometry of the active site from a structure
prediction model like AF3~\cite{abramson2024alphafold3} can have some
predictive power for certain functions. Using inverse folding models to propose
many sequences, it is possible to accumulate millions of potential candidate
sequences which pass all of the filtering metrics, but nearly impossible to
rank which of those will be most functional in an assay readout. Furthermore
the cost of ordering a library with millions of unique sequences is cost
prohibitive.

In this work we will propose a method for designing large scale high fidelity
combinatorial libraries which is cost effective and feasible to generate.
