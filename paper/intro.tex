\subsection*{P1 --- The promise and the bottleneck}
\begin{planlist}
  \item RFdiffusion + MPNN make it possible to design enzyme backbones from

  \item First-pass designs are typically weak; the bottleneck is optimization,

\subsection*{P2 --- Why existing optimization tools fall short}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Site-saturation mutagenesis (SSM): local; cannot escape the
        neighborhood of the starting design.
  \item Vanilla MPNN re-sampling: structurally informed but functionally blind;
        biased toward sequences MPNN's prior favors, not sequences that
        catalyze.
  \item The active-variant region for a de novo backbone often lies tens of
        mutations away --- out of reach of either tool.
  \item Cross-reference Section~\ref{sec:related} for the full treatment;
        this paragraph is the compressed version.
\end{planlist}

\subsection*{P3 --- What we contribute (three orthogonal capabilities)}
\begin{planlist}
  \item Function-aligned RL fine-tuning of ProteinMPNN (GRPO over reward
        pipelines) + fragment-based combinatorial libraries that can physically
        test combinatorially large candidate sets.
  \item \textbf{(i) Method foundation} --- heme oxidation: fine-tuned MPNN
        beats SSM and vanilla MPNN at matched experimental effort.
  \item \textbf{(ii) Closed-loop iteration} --- PETase: an initial experimental
        library trains a surrogate that becomes a reward signal for the next
        library, producing substantially improved designs.
  \item \textbf{(iii) Diversity-aware search} --- protease: a batch-consensus
        mutation-diversity reward accesses rarer mutations and improves
        catalytic activity over an otherwise-matched library.
\end{planlist}
