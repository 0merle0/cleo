\section{Experimental programme and selection algorithms}
\label{sec:experiments}

\plan{This section exists so that every arm, rule and planned experiment is
written down in one place with a fixed name, and so that results reported
elsewhere in the paper can be traced to a defined procedure. Much of it will
move to Materials and Methods at submission; keeping it in the main build for
now makes disagreements visible early.}

\subsection*{Notation}

A design is a sequence $s \in \mathcal{A}^L$ over the 20 canonical amino acids.
Distance is Hamming,
\[
  d(s, t) \;=\; \bigl|\{\, i : s_i \neq t_i \,\}\bigr|,
\]
which is the metric behind every diversity number we report. One-hot encoding
$\phi(s) \in \{0,1\}^{20L}$ satisfies
$\lVert \phi(s) - \phi(t) \rVert_2 = \sqrt{2\, d(s,t)}$, so the PCA projections
in the figures are a linear embedding of exactly this distance rather than a
second, competing notion of similarity. Where an ESM embedding is used instead,
$d$ is replaced by Euclidean distance between mean-pooled representations; this
is reported as a diagnostic and, optionally, as a secondary library metric.

A design \emph{passes} when its motif all-atom RMSD is below
\SI{1.5}{\angstrom} and no backbone atom clashes with the ligand. $\mathcal{P}$
denotes the passing subset of a pool $\mathcal{S}$. The
\emph{anchor} $a$ is the position-wise consensus of low-temperature LigandMPNN
samples --- the single sequence best representing the mode the base model's
likelihood concentrates on.

\subsection*{Selection rules}

\plan{All implemented in \texttt{src/cleo/design/utils/selection.py}, all
sharing one distance backend so the comparison is between rules rather than
between implementations. Benchmarked by
\texttt{paper/figures/ame\_selection\_bench.py}.}

Given a pool $\mathcal{S}$ of $M$ sampled sequences and a budget of $k \ll M$
folds, each rule returns the subset to fold.

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{random}] Uniform without replacement. \textbf{This is the
        control, and it is the bar.} Diversity is trivially won and worthless
        alone: a rule that doubles spread while halving passing designs has made
        the library worse.
  \item[\texttt{maxmin}] Greedy farthest-point (k-center) traversal. Having
        selected $\mathcal{T}$, repeatedly add
        $\arg\max_{s \in \mathcal{S} \setminus \mathcal{T}}
         \min_{t \in \mathcal{T}} d(s,t)$.
        The inner \emph{minimum} is what distinguishes this from max-sum
        selection: it prevents accepting a tight cluster that happens to sit far
        from everything already chosen.
  \item[\texttt{maxmin\_anchor}] As above, scoring
        $w_{\mathrm{self}} \min_{t \in \mathcal{T}} d(s,t) + w_{\mathrm{anchor}}\, d(s,a)$.
        Selects designs novel relative to the incumbent low-temperature
        solution, not merely relative to the rest of the library.
  \item[\texttt{anchor\_far}] Rank by $d(s,a)$ descending. The pure
        ``maximally unlike vanilla MPNN'' rule.
  \item[\texttt{anchor\_band}] Restrict to the shell
        $\{\, s : q_{\mathrm{lo}} \le d(s,a) \le q_{\mathrm{hi}} \,\}$ for
        quantiles of the pool's anchor-distance distribution, then run
        \texttt{maxmin} inside it. Designed as the correction to
        \texttt{maxmin}: bounded above as well as below, so the traversal cannot
        walk out to sequences that are distant because they are degenerate.
  \item[\texttt{cluster\_rep}] Average-linkage clustering cut to $k$ clusters,
        one representative sampled per cluster. Draws from the pool's density
        rather than its edges.
\end{description}

\subsection*{Result: diversity-first acquisition does not work}

\plan{Reported here rather than buried, because it was a starred hypothesis and
because the negative result is informative. Retrospective evaluation on the
M0097 trajectory pool: every sampled sequence already carries a pass label, so
any rule can be scored without folding anything.}

Passing designs recovered, relative to \texttt{random}, at a budget of 400 folds:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Passing designs / random} \\
\midrule
as-sampled          & 2.04 \\
\texttt{random}     & 1.00 \\
\texttt{cluster\_rep}   & 0.40 \\
\texttt{anchor\_band}   & 0.36 \\
\texttt{maxmin}         & 0.36 \\
\texttt{maxmin\_anchor} & 0.24 \\
\texttt{anchor\_far}    & 0.12 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every diversity-directed rule loses to uniform random selection, by factors of
\numrange{2.5}{8}. The mechanism is visible in the spread: no rule moves mean
pairwise distance beyond \numrange{135}{144} residues against random's 135. The
pool is already near-saturated on diversity, so a selection rule has almost
nothing to gain and a great deal to lose --- any systematic deviation from
random correlates with selecting sequences that fail. Banding helps relative to
raw farthest-point traversal ($0.36$ vs.\ $0.07$ at $k = 200$) but does not
approach the control.

\plan{Two honest caveats. The as-sampled row beats random because the pool is
time-ordered and later policies are better --- that is a policy-quality effect,
not a selection effect, and it must not be reported as one. And absolute counts
are small (33 passing in a 1200-design pool), so individual cells are noisy;
what carries the conclusion is that the direction is consistent across six
rules and three budgets.}

\textbf{Consequence for the paper.} Selection is not where the gain is. The
defensible use of these rules is post-hoc: choosing a physically orderable
subset from designs \emph{already known to pass}, where the labels exist and
only the diversity trade-off is live. Deciding what to fold should be left to
the policy.

\subsection*{E21 --- how wide is the run-to-run distribution?}

\plan{Pre-registered. \texttt{random} and \texttt{logprob\_top} on M0097, five
explicit seeds each, 150 steps, oversample 8 --- the E19 config unchanged except
for the seed. Evaluated by the same selection-free harness: \numproduct{96}
samples per run from the final checkpoint, folded best-of-5.}

This is the experiment E19 forced. Two draws of one configuration put the
\texttt{random} control at \SI{68.8}{\percent} and \SI{89.6}{\percent}, a gap
larger than most of the effects the selection experiments were built to detect.
Every pass-rate contrast reported in this slate at $n = 1$ is a difference
measured against noise of unknown width, and no amount of re-reading those
tables fixes that --- the width has to be measured.

Two arms rather than four is deliberate. The quantity wanted is a
\emph{spread}, and a spread is estimated from replicates; a third rule would buy
less than a fourth and fifth seed on the two arms that carry the surviving
claims.

\textbf{Predictions.} The control's five draws span at least 15 points of pass
rate --- if they span far less, E19's 21-point move was an outlier and the
retraction of E18 is too strong. \texttt{logprob\_top}'s distribution overlaps
the control's substantially, so the two arms are not separated by a
between-seed $t$-test at $n = 5$; this is the outcome consistent with everything
after E18 and it makes the pass-rate half of the selection story simply
unresolved at this sample size rather than negative. The $U_{15}$ coverage ratio
stays above 1 in at least four of the five paired seeds --- that is the claim
E19 found stable across six backbones, and if it is also stable across seeds it
is the one result from the selection arc worth carrying into the paper.

\plan{\textbf{What this fixes and what it does not.} It puts an interval on one
cell --- two rules, one backbone, one step count --- and licenses saying how
large an effect this design can resolve. It does not retroactively repair E9,
E16 or E18, whose comparisons remain single draws; what it does is tell the
reader how much weight those can bear. Seeding is new (\texttt{seed:} in the
training config, unset by default), so every run reported before E21 was
unseeded and cannot be re-drawn even in principle.}

\subsection*{Scoring protocol}

Two conventions changed partway through the pilot, and results reported before
the change are not directly comparable to results reported after it.

\textbf{Five diffusion samples everywhere.} The benchmark's unit of success is
a best-of-$N$: a design counts if \emph{any} of its predictions clears the motif
cutoff and is clash-free. Early training runs folded each sequence once
(\texttt{num\_diffusion\_samples: 1}), which understates their success rate
relative to any best-of-$N$ baseline. All runs from this point fold at
\texttt{num\_diffusion\_samples: 5}. Getting this right in training needed a
reduction step, \texttt{rfd2\_benchmark.best\_of\_n\_from\_df}: the oracle's own
collapse keeps the sample AF3 ranks highest by \emph{confidence}, which is not
the benchmark's criterion. The step instead takes the min motif RMSD as the
continuous signal and OR-s the per-prediction conjunction for the pass flag ---
separately reducing ``motif passed'' and ``no clash'' and then AND-ing them
would credit a design for two different predictions and overcount.

\textbf{Metrics are reported on filtered designs.} A library contains only the
designs that survived filtering, so diversity among sequences that failed is
diversity nobody gets to use. Every diversity number below is computed on the
passing subset $\mathcal{P}$, not on everything sampled. Where the two differ
materially the sampled figure is given alongside, because the gap is itself
diagnostic --- it says whether an arm's successes concentrate.

\textbf{Unique mutations.} Mean pairwise Hamming measures how far apart a
library's members are; it does not measure how much of mutational space they
collectively reach. Two libraries can have identical spread while one explores
twice as many distinct substitutions. So we also report
\[
  U(\mathcal{P}) \;=\; \bigl|\{\, (i, a) : \exists\, s \in \mathcal{P},\;
  s_i = a \neq a^{\mathrm{ref}}_i \,\}\bigr|,
\]
the number of distinct (position, residue) substitutions appearing anywhere in
the library, each counted once no matter how many designs carry it. The ceiling
is $19L = 3420$.

$U$ grows with $|\mathcal{P}|$, so arms with different numbers of passing
designs cannot be compared on it directly --- doing so measures sample size as
much as coverage. We rarefy: subsample every arm to a common $k$ designs,
average over 400 draws, and compare at matched $k$, the same construction as a
species-accumulation curve.

\textbf{Per-sequence success rate, not best-of-8.} The published unit (fraction
of backbones with a hit among 8 sequences) saturates and discards resolution:
at $n{=}8$ a rate near \SI{20}{\percent} carries a \SI{95}{\percent} interval of
${\pm}\SI{28}{pp}$, wide enough to swallow every difference in this paper. We
now sample $n{=}96$ per backbone per arm (${\pm}\SI{8}{pp}$) and report the
plain fraction of sequences that succeed. This is a strictly harder number to
move than the best-of-8 statistic, and it shrank the E10 claim --- see below.

\subsection*{E15 --- Centering weight: one knob from centered to diverse}

\plan{No new reward machinery. \texttt{compute\_dist\_to\_ref\_seqs\_from\_df}
already emits Hamming distance to a reference set, and the aggregator already
supports per-metric \texttt{mode}, \texttt{weight} and rank normalisation, so
the whole experiment is a config sweep. Configs generated by
\texttt{experiments/ame/make\_centering\_sweep.py}.}

The reference $a$ is the consensus of low-temperature LigandMPNN samples. Adding

\begin{center}
\texttt{- metric: dist\_to\_ref\_min \quad mode: \{min|max\} \quad weight: $W$ \quad normalize: rank}
\end{center}

to the aggregation gives a single signed axis. Because reward is a weighted
average, $\smash{R = \sum_i w_i r_i / \sum_i w_i}$, the weight is directly
interpretable: $W = 0$ is pure motif RMSD, $W = 1$ gives centering equal
influence to it. \texttt{mode:\ min} pulls the policy toward the incumbent
mode; \texttt{mode:\ max} pushes it away.

Sweep $W \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0\}$ in both directions. Prediction: mean
distance to $a$ falls monotonically under \texttt{min} and rises under
\texttt{max}, and the PCA panels show the passing cloud sliding toward or away
from the low-temperature knot. Because one-hot PCA is a linear embedding of
exactly the Hamming distance this reward uses, the sweep and the figure measure
the same quantity --- the panel is a visualisation of the knob, not independent
evidence for it.

\plan{The knob will move designs; that is not in question and is not the
result. What the experiment has to establish is whether \emph{pass rate
survives} at either end. Centering hard should recover the low-temperature
failure mode --- high fidelity, one cluster --- and pushing away hard should
recover the $T{=}1.0$ failure mode --- broad and dead. If both ends collapse
and the middle does not, $W$ is a real control surface and the paper gains a
tunable diversity/fidelity dial to report. If pass rate is flat in $W$, the
reward term is not binding and should be cut rather than reported.

This also supplies the honest version of the frontier: E10 sweeps the
\emph{baseline's} knob (temperature) and E15 sweeps \emph{ours}. Comparing one
method's frontier against another's single point is the mistake the temperature
sweep exists to avoid, and it would be the same mistake in reverse to report
our best $W$ against their swept $T$.}


\subsection*{E14 result --- the embedding view qualifies the Hamming view}

\plan{Mean-pooled ESM-2 650M, one vector per design, computed for every arm.}

Within a set, ESM distance and Hamming distance are close to \emph{uncorrelated}
($r$ between $-0.17$ and $0.56$ across arms), so the embedding is genuinely
measuring something the Hamming numbers do not. That makes the agreement in
ordering meaningful rather than automatic: on M0097's passing designs,
\cleo{} is more spread than $T{=}0.1$ under both metrics.

But the size of the advantage does not survive the change of metric. The ratio
of \cleo{}'s passing-set spread to the low-temperature arm's is
$\mathbf{2.57\times}$ in Hamming and only $\mathbf{1.31\times}$ in ESM space.
A substantial share of the extra sequence distance \cleo{} buys is
biochemically conservative --- positions that differ in identity but not much
in what ESM considers the residue to be doing.

\plan{This should be stated in the paper, not left for a referee to find.
Reporting ``2.6$\times$ more diverse'' with a metric chosen because it is the
larger number is exactly the move the temperature sweep exists to prevent us
from making about temperature. The defensible claim is that the advantage is
robust to the choice of metric while its magnitude is not, and that the
library-relevant quantity --- distinct passing clusters per fold, where
\cleo{} wins $3.1$ to $1.0$ --- does not depend on either distance at all,
since those designs are separate clusters at any threshold tested.

Caveat on $n$: passing subsets are 5 and 15 designs, so the correlation
figures in particular are noisy. The ratio comparison is the robust part.}

\subsection*{E10 result --- the frontier shift is real but smaller than first reported}

\plan{LigandMPNN sampled at six temperatures, folded best-of-5, and scored by
the same code path as \cleo{}, on M0097. Reported as the plain per-sequence
success rate under the \emph{Scoring protocol} above. The $n$ per arm is
uneven here --- 8 for $T{=}0.1$ and $T{=}1.0$, 24 for the rest, 80 for \cleo{}
--- which is why all six are being resampled at $n{=}96$.}

\textbf{Which set spread is measured over.} Spread is the mean pairwise Hamming
distance --- for every pair in a set, the number of positions at which the two
sequences differ, averaged over all $\binom{n}{2}$ pairs, as a raw substitution
count out of $L = 180$. No threshold, no clustering. The set it is measured over
has to be the \emph{passing} designs, not everything sampled: a library contains
only the designs that survived filtering, so diversity among sequences that
failed is diversity nobody gets to use. Both columns are given below because the
gap between them is itself informative.

\begin{tabular}{@{}lccccc@{}}

\textbf{Spread} & $U_{15}$ \\
\midrule

LigandMPNN $T{=}0.2$ & 96 & \SI{66.7}{\percent} & 64 & 47.8  & 205 \\
LigandMPNN $T{=}0.3$ & 96 & \SI{58.3}{\percent} & 56 & 57.9  & 274 \\

$^{*}$\,The \cleo{} row predates the objective fixes recorded in the algorithm
audit and is a post-hoc peak window; it is provisional until the corrected
200-step arms land. Every baseline row is $n = 96$ on the same code path.

Temperature trades success for diversity, but not monotonically at the low end:
$T{=}0.2$ is the \emph{most} successful arm at \SI{66.7}{\percent}, above
$T{=}0.1$'s \SI{58.3}{\percent}, while also being more diverse. Anyone tuning
only temperature should therefore not be at $T{=}0.1$, and $T{=}0.2$ is the
honest incumbent to beat at the low-diversity end. From $T{=}0.3$ upward the
trade-off is clean and steep: diversity rises 58 to 126 while success falls
\SIrange{58}{4}{\percent}.

\cleo{}'s peak window sits above that curve, by less than the small-$n$ data
suggested:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item at \cleo{}'s diversity (98.8), the baseline succeeds
        \SI{26.7}{\percent}; \cleo{} \SI{37.5}{\percent} --- $\mathbf{1.40\times}$.
  \item at \cleo{}'s success rate (\SI{37.5}{\percent}), the baseline reaches
        spread $75.3$; \cleo{} $98.8$ --- $\mathbf{1.31\times}$.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The rarefied coverage says something the ratios hide.} At a matched 15
passing designs, \cleo{} reaches $U_{15} = 632$ distinct substitutions against
$T{=}0.7$'s 608 --- a factor of $1.04$, which is no advantage at all. Per
passing design \cleo{} explores mutational space at essentially the same rate as
a high-temperature baseline. Its entire edge is that it \emph{passes more often
while doing so}, which is what converts an equal exploration rate into a larger
library for a fixed fold budget. That is a narrower and more defensible claim
than ``more diverse'', and it is the one the data supports.

\plan{\textbf{Three corrections to carry through the rest of the paper.}

First, the earlier $1.75\times$ / $1.46\times$ figures came from averaging over
300 random draws of 8 sequences, the published best-of-8 unit. That statistic
flatters whichever arm has the higher variance across draws. The per-sequence
rate does not, and it is the number to quote.

Second, spread was previously measured over \emph{all} sampled sequences rather
than the passing subset. For the baseline arms this makes almost no difference,
but for \cleo{} it does, and in the flattering direction --- the policy's
failures are scattered while its successes concentrate. All diversity metrics
are now reported on filtered designs only.

Third, the row previously labelled ``\cleo{} peak'' pooled two disjoint training
windows, steps 36--45 and 190--199. The second contributes 40 sequences and one
success, so pooling halved the reported success rate (\SIrange{37.5}{20.0}{\percent})
while inflating spread with the drift \emph{between} the windows
(\numrange{103.1}{132.0}) --- an artefact of the same PC1-versus-step drift
documented in the PCA panel. The windows are reported separately above.

The two errors partly cancelled, which is why the pooled row looks superficially
reasonable. Corrected on the right set and the right window, the peak-window
advantage is $1.7\times$ in success at matched diversity --- larger than the
$1.26\times$ the pooled row implied, and resting on a different comparison.

\textbf{What is not yet settled.} Using the peak window is a post-hoc choice
unless the stopping rule is fixed in advance; E12's ${\sim}75$-step rule does
contain it, but that rule was set after seeing these runs. The
trajectory-as-library framing instead takes the whole run, which is the pooled
row --- and there the extra spread is drift, not diversity available from any
one policy. These are different libraries answering different questions and the
results section must not quote one number for both. The $n{=}96$ rerun is what
gives either claim usable error bars: the passing subsets here are 15 designs
for \cleo{} and 5 for $T{=}0.7$.}

36--45 and is essentially dead by step 190. Diagnosis on the full trajectory,
20-step bins.}

\textbf{Mechanism: the reward's dynamic range is misallocated.} With
normalisation bounds $[0.5, 20.0]$ on motif RMSD, a passing design at
\SI{1.4}{\angstrom} scores $0.954$ and a clearly failing one at
\SI{2.0}{\angstrom} scores $0.923$. The entire pass/fail distinction is worth
$0.031$ of reward, against a within-batch standard deviation of $0.126$ that is
dominated by rare catastrophic folds scoring near zero. GRPO divides its
group-relative advantage by exactly that standard deviation. So the gradient
answers ``did this batch contain a blow-up'', a question that is settled by
step ${\sim}20$ once median RMSD falls to \SI{2.1}{\angstrom}, and thereafter
carries almost no information about the boundary where all the value is. With
\texttt{kl\_weight: 0.0} nothing anchors the policy once the signal goes flat,
and it random-walks.

\textbf{Rank normalisation fixes it.} Replacing value normalisation with
within-batch rank (\texttt{normalize: rank}) makes the advantage invariant to
how compressed the raw values are: the \SI{1.4}{\angstrom} and
\SI{2.0}{\angstrom} designs are adjacent in RMSD but a full rank step apart, so
the pass/fail ordering always yields gradient, and a \SI{37}{\angstrom} blow-up
is merely rank 0 rather than an outlier that sets the batch scale.

so E12's ${\sim}75$-step early-stopping rule was fitted to the \emph{broken}
configuration and should not be carried over. A 200-step rank-normalised run is
in flight.

\plan{\textbf{Two implementation findings this turned up, both of which affect
how these runs should be read.}

\emph{Reward logging is vacuous under rank normalisation.} With a single metric,
rank maps the batch to a fixed uniform distribution on $[0,1]$, so mean reward
is exactly $0.5$ at every step by construction --- measured across all 75 steps
of the E15 arms, $\mathrm{std} = 0$ to float precision. ``Best reward seen:
0.5000'' in those logs is not a coincidence, and any checkpoint selection or
early stopping keyed on reward is selecting an arbitrary step. Progress must be
tracked on the raw metric (median motif RMSD, pass rate) instead.

\emph{The KL penalty is gated on \texttt{use\_ref\_kl}, not on
\texttt{kl\_weight}.} Setting a non-zero \texttt{kl\_weight} without
\texttt{use\_ref\_kl: true} silently does nothing --- no reference model is even
loaded. The estimator itself is correct where it does run: $\hat{k}_3 = \rho -
\log\rho - 1$ with $\rho = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}/\pi_\theta$, non-negative and
applied with the right sign.

\textbf{A third finding, not yet acted on, because fixing it invalidates every
run above.} The clipped surrogate computes $\min\bigl(\sum_t \rho_t A,
\sum_t \mathrm{clip}(\rho_t) A\bigr)$ --- a minimum of sums --- where PPO and
GRPO specify $\sum_t \min\bigl(\rho_t A, \mathrm{clip}(\rho_t) A\bigr)$, a sum
of minima. These are not equal, and the difference is not cosmetic: the
implemented form makes one clip/no-clip decision for an entire 180-token
sequence, so a single token past the clip boundary switches every other token in
that sequence onto the clipped branch and zeroes its gradient. On a two-token
example with $\rho = (5.0, 0.1)$ and $A > 0$, the correct objective is $1.38$
with gradient $(0, 0.1)$; the implemented one gives $2.08$ with gradient
$(0, 0)$. With $L = 180$ the clip is triggered by some token almost always, so
the trust region is applied far more bluntly than intended. This is a plausible
additional contributor to the drift, independent of the reward-scaling problem,
and it should be fixed --- but it changes the objective, so every result in this
section would need regenerating.}

\subsection*{Algorithm audit}

\plan{A line-by-line read of \texttt{grpo.py} against the GRPO/DAPO
specifications, prompted by the E17 divergence. Recorded in full because two of
the findings change the objective, and any result in this section predating a
fix has to be read in that light.}

\textbf{Verified correct.} The reference-KL estimator is the standard
low-variance form $\hat{k}_3 = \rho - \log\rho - 1$ with
$\rho = \pi_{\mathrm{ref}}/\pi_\theta$, non-negative and applied with the right
sign. $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$ is stored from the rollout and the update is
teacher-forced through the same decoding order and sampled actions, so the
importance ratio is exactly 1 on the first inner update, as it must be. The
encoder is deliberately frozen --- only \texttt{decoder\_layers} and
\texttt{W\_out} receive gradient --- and computing the encoding under
\texttt{no\_grad} is consistent with that rather than an omission. Asymmetric
clipping follows DAPO.

\textbf{Finding 1: the trust region is applied per sequence, not per token.}
The surrogate computes $\min\bigl(\sum_t \rho_t A, \sum_t \mathrm{clip}(\rho_t) A\bigr)$
where PPO and GRPO specify $\sum_t \min\bigl(\rho_t A, \mathrm{clip}(\rho_t) A\bigr)$.
A minimum of sums is not a sum of minima: the implemented form takes one
clip/no-clip decision for an entire $L = 180$ token sequence, so a single token
past the boundary switches every other token onto the clipped branch and zeroes
its gradient. On $\rho = (5.0, 0.1)$ with $A > 0$ the correct objective is
$1.38$ with gradient $(0, 0.1)$; the implemented one gives $2.08$ with gradient
$(0,0)$. With 180 tokens some token triggers the clip nearly always.

\textbf{Finding 2: the advantage is normalised over the wrong group.} GRPO's
defining feature is that the baseline is the mean reward over the group sampled
from one prompt. Here the advantage is recomputed inside the inner loop on each
randomly drawn sub-batch of \texttt{update\_batch\_size} $= 8$ out of $B = 16$,
rather than once over the full rollout. The same sequence therefore receives a
different advantage on each of the 16 inner updates, and sequences near the
median receive advantages of \emph{both signs} --- 3 of 16 in a representative
draw --- so the update pushes the same rollout up and down within one step. The
fix is to compute the advantage once, before the loop.

\textbf{Finding 3: no length normalisation.} $\sum_t \rho_t$ scales with
sequence length, and the sum runs over all $L$ positions including residues held
fixed by \texttt{fixed\_residues}, which contribute $\rho \approx 1$ each. The
effective step size therefore depends on how long the protein is and how much of
it is designable. The standard form is a mean over designed tokens.

\textbf{Deeper pass: the rest of the loop.} A second audit covering the rollout,
the training loop and checkpointing found the following.

\emph{Clean.} Dropout is constructed at $p = 0$ throughout, so
\texttt{model.train()} injects no stochasticity and the importance ratio is
genuinely 1 on the first inner update rather than only nominally so. Sampling
probabilities are taken from \texttt{logits.detach()}, correctly keeping the
sampling step out of the gradient. Row counts in the per-step metrics are a
constant 16 across all 275 logged steps of the two completed runs, so no fold
failure has silently dropped a design; were one to, the inner merge would
shorten the reward vector and the indexing would raise rather than misalign.

\emph{Finding 4 (\textbf{now fixed}): the behaviour policy is not the policy
being scored.} Actions
are drawn from $\mathrm{softmax}\bigl((\ell + b)/T\bigr)$ renormalised over the
20 residue tokens, where $b = -10^{8}$ at amino acids excluded by
\texttt{omit\_AA}. The log-probabilities used for the ratio are
$\log\mathrm{softmax}(\ell)$ over all 21 tokens --- no bias, no temperature. The
two differ by $\log P(\text{allowed})$, which depends on $\theta$ and therefore
does not cancel between $\pi_{\mathrm{new}}$ and $\pi_{\mathrm{old}}$: the true
ratio is the implemented one times
$P_{\mathrm{old}}(\text{allowed}) / P_{\mathrm{new}}(\text{allowed})$. While the
mass the model places on C and X stays put this factor is 1 and nothing is
wrong; if the policy drifts mass onto the omitted tokens --- which nothing in
the objective discourages, since they are merely never sampled --- the error
grows with the drift and is not currently monitored. A policy putting
\SI{2}{\percent} on omitted tokens where it once put \SI{1}{\percent} carries a
\SI{1}{\percent} systematic error in every ratio.

The same mismatch makes \texttt{temperature} a trap rather than a knob. At
$T = 1$ the scored and sampled log-probabilities differ by ${\sim}0.05$ nats; at
$T = 0.5$ by $1.3$; at $T = 0.1$ by $17.8$. Training at any temperature other
than 1 silently optimises a different distribution from the one it samples.
\texttt{CLAUDE.md} already advises keeping $T = 1$ to avoid mode collapse; this
is a second and stronger reason.

Scoring now uses
$\log\mathrm{softmax}\bigl(((\ell + b)/T)_{1:20}\bigr)$, padded to 21
channels --- exactly the distribution actions are drawn from, verified equal to
the true sampling log-probability to float precision at $T \in \{1, 0.5, 0.1\}$
where the previous form was off by $0.17$, $4.3$ and $44.2$ nats respectively.
The pre-fix behaviour is retained behind \texttt{legacy\_logprobs} for the
ablation. Separately, every run now logs \texttt{p\_omit}, the probability mass
the policy places on tokens \texttt{omit\_AA} forbids, since that is precisely
the quantity whose drift makes the omitted correction bite and nothing in the
objective holds it down.

\emph{Finding 5: the final policy was never saved.} \texttt{\_last.pt} was
written only inside the ``every $n$ steps'' branch, so a 75-step run left it at
step 50 and a 200-step run would leave it at 175. Confirmed directly from the
E15 artefacts: the four checkpoints on disk are steps 25, 50, \texttt{\_best}
(25) and \texttt{\_last} (50), for a run that trained to step 74. Under rank
normalisation those runs were still improving when they ended, so the discarded
tail was the best part of the run.

\emph{Finding 6: \texttt{\_best.pt} selects on a constant.} Best-checkpoint
selection compared \texttt{to\_log["reward"]}, which under a rank-normalised
single-metric reward is exactly $0.5$ at every step. With a strict $>$ test the
first checkpoint written wins permanently --- which is why every E15 arm reports
its best at step 25 or 50 and never later. \textbf{Both are fixed}:
\texttt{\_last.pt} is now written after the loop at the true final step, and
selection runs on a configurable \texttt{checkpoint\_metric}
(\texttt{ame\_motif\_rmsd\_batch\_mean}, mode \texttt{min}) with a warning
emitted if the tracked quantity never varies.

\plan{\textbf{All three are now fixed}, and the pre-fix objective is retained
behind \texttt{legacy\_surrogate: true} so the fix can be measured rather than
merely asserted. The surrogate takes a per-token minimum and averages it over
designed tokens only --- \texttt{chain\_mask} is zero at fixed residues, whose
log-probs the rollout already zeroes, so including them would contribute
$\rho = 1$ apiece and make the step size depend on how much of the protein is
held fixed, which disposes of Finding 3 as well. The advantage is computed once
over the full rollout group before the update loop.

\textbf{The A/B ladder.} Four 200-step arms on M0097, each turning on exactly
one more fix than the last, so any effect can be attributed:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Arm} & \textbf{Surrogate} & \textbf{Log-probs} & \textbf{KL} \\
\midrule
\texttt{conv200\_legacy} & legacy & legacy & --- \\
\texttt{conv200\_surr}   & fixed  & legacy & --- \\
\texttt{conv200}         & fixed  & fixed  & --- \\

\end{center}

All four log \texttt{p\_omit} and all four checkpoint on

The KL gating defect noted under E17 \emph{has} been fixed --- a non-zero

\subsection*{E16 result --- selecting for diversity during training also fails}

\plan{The prospective version of E9. Two 75-step arms on M0097, identical but

\numproduct{64} get folded: uniform random, or greedy farthest-point
(\texttt{maxmin}). Best-of-5 folding, so pass rates here are on the benchmark's
unit and are not comparable to the single-sample E15 numbers.}

$U$ & $U_{50}$ \\
\midrule

The rule does exactly what it is designed to do and the design is still wrong.
Per passing design, \texttt{maxmin} genuinely explores more mutational space ---
$U_{50}$ of 1486 against 1041, a factor of $1.43$ at matched design count, and a
spread of 121.6 against 93.3. It simply produces \emph{eight times fewer} such
designs, so the library actually obtained covers 1545 distinct substitutions
against random's 2423. Diversity per design is up $1.43\times$; total library
coverage is down $1.57\times$.

This is the same conclusion as E9 and a stronger version of it. E9 selected
retrospectively from a finished pool, where the only cost was picking sequences
that happened to fail. Selecting during training adds a second cost on top:
because the selection depends on the sampled actions, the batch GRPO computes
its group-relative baseline over is no longer a sample from the policy, so the
importance ratios apply to a distribution that was never sampled --- the
gradient is biased by construction, as the docstring on \texttt{collect\_rollout}
warns. The \SI{36.7}{\percent} versus \SI{4.6}{\percent} gap is far too large to
be selection cost alone.

\textbf{Consequence.} Both the retrospective and the prospective experiment now
say the same thing: deciding what to fold should be left to the policy. The
defensible use of these rules remains post-hoc, choosing an orderable subset from
designs already known to pass.

\textbf{Re-measured without selection, the result stands.} The table above is a
pass rate over \emph{folded} designs, which E18 later showed cannot be compared
across selection rules --- so the conclusion was re-derived on the one
measurement that can be. Both checkpoints were sampled \numproduct{96} times with
no selection applied and folded best-of-5.

\subsection*{E18 --- was E16's negative about selection, or about selecting on
distance?}

\plan{Pre-registered. Six 150-step arms on M0097, identical but for the rule
used to pick which \numproduct{16} of an oversampled pool of \numproduct{128}
get folded. Predictions are recorded here \emph{before} the runs finish so the
result can falsify them rather than be read post hoc.}

E16 established that picking designs to fold by farthest-point traversal is
much worse than picking them at random --- \SI{4.6}{\percent} passing against
\SI{36.7}{\percent}. But it lost for a diagnosable reason: \texttt{maxmin} is
drawn to sequences that are distant \emph{because they are degenerate}. On a
synthetic pool built with a degenerate low-probability tail, \SI{94}{\percent}
of its picks come from that tail. That diagnosis implicates the axis, not the
idea: every rule available to E16 scored on sequence distance alone, and the
policy's own log-probability of each sampled sequence --- computed during the
rollout, free to reuse --- is a quality signal none of them could see.

\textbf{The bias is instrumented rather than corrected.} As in E16 the selected
batch is not a draw from the policy, so the importance ratios apply to a
distribution that was never sampled. Two diagnostics now record what that
costs. \texttt{sel\_logp\_shift} is how far the rule moved the batch in the
policy's own measure --- the pool's rewards are never observed, since only the
\numproduct{16} are folded, so a selected-versus-pool \emph{reward} gap is not
computable and this is the honest substitute. \texttt{reward\_std} is the
spread the group-relative advantage actually divides by, and it is the test of
whether a rule bought the thing it was chosen for, independently of whether the
run ends up better.

\plan{Cost note: folding is \numproduct{16} designs $\times$ 5 diffusion
samples per step regardless of the oversample factor, so runtime scales with
\texttt{N\_steps} and not with the pool. Raising \numrange{64}{128} is close to
free.}

\subsection*{E18 --- two design errors, caught mid-run}

Both are recorded rather than quietly repaired, because one of them invalidates
a rationale stated above and the other would have produced a headline number
that is pure artefact.

\textbf{1. \texttt{reward\_std} is degenerate under rank normalisation.} It was
added as the test of whether a rule bought the reward spread it was chosen for.
It cannot be: with \texttt{normalize: rank} the reward is the within-batch rank
mapped to $[0,1]$, so its standard deviation is fixed by the batch size alone.
Measured, it is \num{0.317397} in \emph{every arm at every step} --- two or
three distinct values across an entire run, all from tie-averaging --- against
a theoretical \num{0.3073} for 16 evenly spaced ranks.

This is not merely a dead diagnostic. The stated rationale for
\texttt{logprob\_strat} was that it targets reward spread within the group,
which is what the group-relative baseline divides by. Under rank normalisation
that spread is \emph{already} constant, so the rule cannot be operating through
the mechanism it was designed around. Whatever it is doing, it is not that.
Testing the mechanism as written would require an arm with value normalisation,
where the spread is free to vary.

\textbf{2. The pass rate over folded designs cannot be compared across arms.}
It is the obvious summary and it is confounded by construction: each rule
chooses which \numproduct{16} of \numproduct{128} get folded, so a rule that
selects for quality reports a high pass rate whether or not its policy improved
at all. \texttt{logprob\_top} selects the 16 most probable sequences and reports
\SI{78.3}{\percent} against \texttt{random}'s \SI{25.2}{\percent}; almost all of
that gap is the selection, not the policy.

This is the same trap as the \texttt{as-sampled} row in E9, running the other
way. There it was a policy-quality effect masquerading as a selection effect;
here it is a selection effect masquerading as policy quality. Entropy over
folded designs is confounded for the same reason --- \texttt{maxmin} selects for
diversity, so of course its folded set is diverse.

\plan{\textbf{Consequence.} E18 cannot be read off the training logs at all.
The comparison has to be made by sampling from each final checkpoint
\emph{without selection} and folding that, which is the only measurement in
which the arms differ solely by the policy they produced. The training-log
columns retained below are the ones that are not confounded:
\texttt{sel\_logp\_shift}, which is a property of the rule rather than of the
outcome, and which confirms every rule does what it claims --- \num{+29.7} nats
for \texttt{logprob\_top}, \num{-19.2} and \num{-18.3} for \texttt{maxmin} and
\texttt{cluster\_rep}, and \num{-0.04} and \num{-0.34} for the stratified and
banded rules, which move the batch almost not at all in the policy's own
measure.}

\textbf{The pre-registered mechanism is falsified, by the test written down for
it.} \texttt{logprob\_top} was predicted to fold excellent designs and stall
anyway, with the explicit rider that if it trained well the group-baseline story
was wrong. It trains well: it is the one arm that beats \texttt{random} on
\emph{both} axes, \SI{82.3}{\percent} against \SI{68.8}{\percent} ($p = 0.043$)
at $U_{15}$ 603 against 558. Pure exploitation, the rule with the least reward
spread available to it, is not merely survivable but dominant over the control.
This is the same conclusion the \texttt{reward\_std} defect reached from the
other direction: under rank normalisation the group's spread is constant by
construction, so no rule could have been operating through it.

\texttt{logprob\_strat}, predicted best, is half right in a way that matters.
It does take the highest pass rate, \SI{87.5}{\percent} ($p = 0.003$), and it is
the largest single effect in the arm. But it is the \emph{only} rule whose
coverage falls below the control on both measures --- $U$ 1141 against 1237,
$U_{15}$ 525 against 558. It bought its pass rate by collapsing the policy,
which is precisely the trade the whole method exists to refuse. It was also not
the only rule to beat random, the other half of the prediction.

\textbf{The two distance rules did not lose.} \texttt{maxmin} was predicted to
lose again and harder; it is indistinguishable from the control on pass rate
(\SI{70.8}{\percent}, $p = 0.88$) while carrying higher coverage.
\texttt{cluster\_rep} takes the lowest pass rate, \SI{56.2}{\percent}, but the
gap does not reach significance ($p = 0.10$) and it returns the highest per-design
coverage in the arm, $U_{15}$ 746 --- a factor of \num{1.34} over the control.
Neither is E16's eight-fold catastrophe. They trade along a frontier rather than
falling off it.

\textbf{\texttt{logprob\_band} is the rule that worked as designed}, and the
diagnostic shows the mechanism directly. It was built as the targeted repair of
E16: keep the diversity objective, remove the degenerate tail it was drawn to.
Its \texttt{sel\_logp\_shift} is \num{-0.3}, so it moves the batch essentially
not at all in the policy's own measure --- but its selected log-probability
spread is \num{8.7} against the pool's \num{18.2}, so it is exploring inside a
truncated band rather than walking to the edge. The outcome matches: $U_{15}$ up
\SI{27}{\percent} over the control at no pass-rate cost that the data can resolve
($p = 0.76$).

\textbf{What survives.} Selection at $128 \to 16$ is not the dead end E16 made it
look. The control is not on the frontier --- \texttt{logprob\_top} dominates it
outright, and \texttt{logprob\_band} buys a quarter more coverage for a pass-rate
difference the experiment cannot resolve. What is dead is the reason we expected
it to work.

\plan{\textbf{What this cannot support.} One run per arm. The binomial interval
on a pass rate at $n = 96$ is roughly $\pm 9$ points before seed variance is
added, and the A/B replicates put seed variance on top of that --- so the three
arms with $p > 0.1$ are genuinely unresolved, not tied. The coverage ordering
spans $1.42\times$ (525 to 746), comfortably outside the bootstrap noise scale of
$\pm 25$--40; note that a bootstrap of a distinct-count statistic is biased
\emph{downward}, since resampling with replacement introduces duplicates that can
only reduce $U$, so those intervals are read for width and not for position.
Replicating \texttt{logprob\_band} and \texttt{logprob\_top} at $n = 3$ is the
cheap next step and the one that would license a recommendation.

\textbf{The open question this reopens.} E16 concluded that \texttt{maxmin}
damages the policy. That conclusion rests on a pass rate over folded designs ---
the exact quantity error 2 above shows cannot be compared across selection rules.
Both E16 checkpoints survive, so the same selection-free harness settles it
directly; that evaluation is running. If E16's maxmin policy is fine and only its
folded set was bad, E16's headline needs rewriting to a claim about wasted folding
compute rather than about policy damage.}

\subsection*{E19 --- does the frontier belong to the rules, or to M0097?}

\plan{Pre-registered. Four rules --- \texttt{random}, \texttt{logprob\_top},
\texttt{logprob\_band}, \texttt{logprob\_strat} --- on three backbones, 150 steps,
oversample 8, evaluated by the same selection-free harness E18 required. The two
distance rules are dropped: neither separated from the control on pass rate, and
E16's re-evaluation covers \texttt{maxmin} directly.}

Nothing seeds the trainer explicitly, so re-running M0097 in a fresh process is
an independent draw rather than a repeat. That makes the M0097 arm a seed
replicate of E18 and the M0904 and M0907 arms a test of generalisation, and the
two failure modes are distinguishable: if M0097 reproduces and the other two do
not, the frontier is a property of that backbone; if M0097 itself does not
reproduce, E18 was noise.

\textbf{Predictions.} \texttt{logprob\_top} continues to dominate
\texttt{random} on both axes --- this is E18's one unambiguous positive and the
claim most worth breaking. \texttt{logprob\_band} continues to buy coverage at
no resolvable pass cost. \texttt{logprob\_strat} continues to raise pass rate
while falling below the control on coverage; it is included precisely because a
collapse result is the counterweight to the headline, and a headline whose
counterweight does not replicate is not worth reporting either.

\plan{Three backbones give three paired contrasts per rule rather than three
draws of one number, which is the stronger design --- but it is still $n = 1$
per cell. The arm can show that a direction is consistent across backbones; it
cannot put a tight interval on the size of the effect on any one of them.}

that mattered. This is the branch the pre-registration named: E18 was noise.

in this paper at $n \approx 96$ is under-powered, not merely imprecise. The
binomial interval we quoted for E18 ($\pm 9$ points) described sampling error
inside one run and silently omitted run-to-run variance, which is the larger
term. Every $n = 1$ pass-rate comparison in the slate inherits this, and the fix
is replication, not a wider interval on one draw.}

real frontier here, but it is a \emph{trade} --- coverage bought with pass rate

consistent, and consistently mediocre: coverage up \numrange{1.24}{1.34}$\times$
on all three E19 backbones, pass rate down on all three.

that worked as designed. Across three fresh backbones it takes
\SI{64.6}{\percent}, \SI{9.4}{\percent} and \SI{0.0}{\percent} --- on M0907 not a
single one of 96 designs passes, with median motif RMSD at \SI{2.07}{\angstrom}
against a \SI{1.5}{\angstrom} cutoff. Its sequence-level diversity was the most
stable quantity we measured, \numrange{102}{107} mean pairwise Hamming on all six
backbones, and it is precisely that stability that turns out to be the warning:
the band holds the policy at a fixed spread in its own log-probability measure
whether or not that spread is anywhere near the motif. Diversity decoupled from
the objective is not exploration, and the interim sequence-level table we
reported before folding pointed the wrong way for exactly this reason.

\subsection*{E20 --- can selection rescue a backbone the benchmark scores 0/40?}

\plan{Pre-registered. \texttt{random}, \texttt{logprob\_band} and
\texttt{logprob\_top} on M0050, M0365 and M0375 --- the three near-misses from
E3, all scored 0/40 by the published benchmark. The matched \texttt{random}
control at the same oversample is the comparator, not E3's numbers, which differ
in both step count and oversample.}

This is a different regime from E18 and the distinction is the whole point of
running it. E18 measured these rules where \SIrange{56}{88}{\percent} of designs
already pass; these backbones sit near \SIrange{0.1}{0.5}{\percent}, with median
motif RMSD of \SIrange{2.8}{3.1}{\angstrom} against a \SI{1.5}{\angstrom} cutoff
and every E3 hit an excursion into the tail rather than a shifted mode.

\textbf{The mechanistic reason to expect little.} A selection rule chooses among
the designs the policy already produced. If nothing in the pool is close, no
choice among them manufactures a hit --- selection re-ranks, it does not
generate. On a backbone whose median sits \SI{1.4}{\angstrom} outside the cutoff,
the pool is mostly not close.

\textbf{The reason to run it anyway.} The quantity that has to move is the
median, not the pass rate, and \texttt{logprob\_band}'s effect is on coverage:
it keeps the policy exploring distinct regions instead of walking to the
degenerate edge, which is more places to find a mode. So the pre-registered
prediction is a split one --- \emph{pass rate does not move on any of the three,
and if anything moves it is median RMSD under \texttt{logprob\_band}}. Pass rate
is close to useless as a readout here in any case: at these rates a 150-step run
returns single-digit counts, so median and minimum motif RMSD are the primary
endpoints and the pass count is reported only for continuity with E3.

\plan{A null here is publishable and worth stating in advance as such: it would
establish that the E18 frontier is a property of the high-pass-rate regime and
does not transfer to hard targets, which bounds the claim rather than weakening
it. The failure mode to guard against is reading a one- or two-design difference
in a 1200-fold pool as a rescue --- E3's replication at $N = 40$ already showed
that four of its seven hits do not survive refolding.}

1/40, exactly the tail draw its \SI{0.023}{\angstrom} margin predicted; neither
the M0050 nor the M0375 rescue survives and both should be dropped. Margin at
training time predicts replication well: the only design with a margin above
\SI{0.03}{\angstrom} is the only one that reproduces.

\plan{\textbf{The general lesson matters more than the rescue.} These seven were
selected \emph{because} they passed, so they are the winners of a noisy filter
and regression on refolding is expected --- but the size of it is the point. A
design passing 1/40 of the time is flagged by a best-of-5 training filter about
\SI{12}{\percent} of the time it is folded, so a best-of-5 label is a weak
indicator of reproducible success, and the in-training pass rates reported
throughout this section (\SI{83}{\percent} for the corrected arm,
\SI{57}{\percent} for the anchored one) are counts of best-of-5 events rather
than of designs that would survive this test.

They are not \emph{wrong} --- best-of-$N$ is the benchmark's own definition, and
by that definition four of these seven clear it where the published run cleared
none. But the two quantities should not be conflated, and any headline number
that leaves this section ought to be replicated the way \texttt{hit00} was. The
cheap version is to raise \texttt{num\_diffusion\_samples} at evaluation time
only; the honest version is to report both.

Still open: the compute is not matched (E4), and the idealised-versus-unidealised
reference-structure question still blocks M0904 and M0907.}

\subsection*{A/B result --- the fixes work, and the failure mode inverts}

\plan{Four 200-step arms on M0097, each one fix further than the last, all with
rank normalisation and best-of-5 folding.}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccrcrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Arm} & \textbf{Success} & \textbf{Entropy} & \textbf{Spread} &
$U$ & $U_{15}$ & \textbf{succ.} & \textbf{div.} \\
\midrule
legacy objective   & \SI{78.0}{\percent} & 0.88 & 69.8  & 1879 & 467 & $1.71\times$ & $1.21\times$ \\
$+$ surrogate fix  & \SI{88.8}{\percent} & 1.09 & 82.6  & 2286 & 575 & $2.65\times$ & $1.43\times$ \\
$+$ log-prob fix   & \SI{83.1}{\percent} & 0.99 & 76.6  & 2109 & 510 & $2.16\times$ & $1.32\times$ \\
$+$ KL anchor      & \SI{56.7}{\percent} & \textbf{1.65} & \textbf{114.4} &
  \textbf{2501} & \textbf{808} & $\mathbf{3.98\times}$ & $\mathbf{1.93\times}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

All four arms completed 200 steps; statistics are over their final 40 (640
designs each), with ratios against the $n = 96$ baseline frontier interpolated
on its monotone branch ($T \ge 0.3$).

\textbf{The divergence is gone.} Every corrected arm converges: success climbs
monotonically and stays climbing, where the original run peaked at
\SI{13.8}{\percent} near step 40 and decayed to zero. Even the
\texttt{legacy\_surrogate} arm converges, because it still carries rank
normalisation --- which confirms the E17 diagnosis that the reward's compressed
dynamic range, not the surrogate, was the primary cause of the collapse. The
surrogate and advantage bugs cost real performance on top of it:
\SIrange{78.0}{88.8}{\percent} success, and a $1.71\times$ to $2.65\times$ jump
in the frontier ratio.

\textbf{That last comparison was held back pending replication, and replication
killed it.} Training is unseeded --- no \texttt{manual\_seed} anywhere in the
pipeline --- so every run is a different trajectory, and the table above has
$n = 1$ per arm. Two further replicates of each of the two contested arms give
$n = 3$ on exactly that comparison:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Arm ($n=3$)} & \textbf{Success} & \textbf{Entropy} & $U$ & $U_{15}$ \\
\midrule
$+$ surrogate fix (no log-prob fix) & $86.0 \pm 4.7$ & $1.06 \pm 0.04$ &
  $2225 \pm 57$ & $544 \pm 31$ \\
$+$ log-prob fix                    & $88.3 \pm 4.9$ & $0.92 \pm 0.10$ &

\midrule
Welch $p$                           & 0.60 & 0.13 & 0.036 & 0.19 \\
\bottomrule

The \SIrange{88.8}{83.1}{\percent} gap was seed noise, and it does not merely
shrink --- it \emph{reverses} sign, to \SI{88.3}{\percent} against
\SI{86.0}{\percent}, at $p = 0.60$. Within-arm spread is ${\pm}5$ points, which
is larger than the effect it was being asked to support. Refusing to report that
difference was correct.

What survives is weaker and points the other way: the log-prob fix costs total
coverage, $U$ \numrange{2225}{2074} at a nominal $p = 0.036$. That is one of four
metrics tested and does not survive a multiplicity correction, so it is a hint
rather than a result --- but every diversity column leans the same direction and
none of them favours the fix.

\textbf{The log-prob fix is nonetheless kept, because it is a correctness fix
and not a performance one.} It makes the scored distribution match the
distribution actually sampled from; without it the ratio in the surrogate is not
a likelihood ratio between the two policies, so the clipping bounds do not mean
what PPO/DAPO says they mean and the objective is not the one we claim to
optimise. A known-wrong gradient is not made right by scoring two points higher
on one metric, and given the measured seed variance those two points are not
even evidence. This is worth stating plainly because it is the one place in the
audit where the corrected code does not beat the buggy code on the headline
number.

\plan{Whether the coverage cost is real needs $n \gtrsim 8$ per arm to resolve,
which is not currently worth the GPU time: the KL anchor moves $U_{15}$ by
\num{300}, and this effect, if it exists at all, is \num{60}.}

\textbf{But the failure mode inverts.} Entropy no longer rises; it falls, from
${\sim}1.7$ to 1.06 in the fully corrected arm. Having fixed a policy that
diffused outward until it was useless, we now have one that converges onto a
narrow solution: \SI{79.2}{\percent} success at spread 80.1 and $U_{15} = 518$,
which is \emph{below} $T{=}0.7$'s 608. On per-design coverage the corrected
policy without an anchor is worse than a high-temperature baseline. Crucially,
this is true of \emph{both} corrected arms and all six replicates --- mean
$U_{15}$ is 544 and 485, both well under 608 --- so it is a property of the
fixed objective rather than of any one run. Sharpening the gradient does not
rescue it: no version of the correct objective, anchored or not, explores enough
on its own.

\textbf{The KL anchor is the knob against it.} At
$\texttt{kl\_weight} = 0.02$ entropy stays flat across the whole run (1.68 to
1.65) instead of collapsing, and the library is the best of any arm: $U_{15} =
782$, \num{1.51} times the unanchored arm and \num{1.29} times $T{=}0.7$, at
spread 110.9. Over its final 40 steps the anchored arm
reaches \SI{56.7}{\percent} success at spread 114.4 with $U_{15} = 808$ ---
$\mathbf{3.98\times}$ the baseline success at matched diversity and
$\mathbf{1.93\times}$ the baseline diversity at matched success --- against the
unanchored arm's \SI{83.1}{\percent} at spread 76.6, $U_{15} = 510$
($2.16\times$ / $1.32\times$). Entropy settles at 1.65 versus 0.99.

\textbf{What this changes about the paper.} \texttt{kl\_weight} is the
diversity control the narrative needs, and it is better behaved than E15's
centering weight: it holds entropy open without requiring a reference sequence
to centre on. The obvious next experiment is a sweep of it, which is E13's slot.

\subsection*{E13 result --- the anchor has an interior optimum, and 0.02 is in it}

Both caveats above are now settled. The anchored arm did keep improving: run to
200 steps it reaches \SI{56.7}{\percent} over its final 40, not the
\SI{42.5}{\percent} measured mid-climb, so the cost of anchoring is roughly half
what that snapshot implied. And the sweep answers where to put $w$.

Two quantities move in opposite directions and it matters which one the library
cares about. Success rate falls monotonically, \SIrange{83}{10}{\percent}.
Per-design coverage $U_{15}$ rises monotonically, \numrange{510}{881}. Neither
is the objective on its own: what a library needs is the \emph{total} number of
distinct substitutions it actually contains, and that is $U$, which is the
product of the two effects and therefore \textbf{unimodal}. It is flat within
noise across $w \in [0.01, 0.05]$ --- 2498, 2501, 2480 --- and falls off a cliff
at $w = 0.1$, where the anchor is strong enough that only \SI{10}{\percent} of
designs pass and there are too few of them to cover anything, despite each one
being the most exploratory in the sweep.

So the answer to the question E17 left open is that \num{0.02} is in the
plateau, not on a slope, and the choice within $[0.01, 0.05]$ is a genuine free
parameter rather than a tuned one --- it buys per-design coverage
(\numrange{760}{858}) with success rate (\SIrange{58}{41}{\percent}) at
constant total yield. \num{0.02} is a defensible default because it sits in the
middle of the flat region.

\textbf{Read the $w{=}0.1$ arm as a failure, not as an endpoint.} Its trajectory
does not converge: binned pass rate runs 4.5, 5.0, 6.2, 7.8, 7.0, 11.5, 15.5,
6.5 --- it is still wandering at step 200. The anchor is strong enough to
prevent the policy from moving, so its high entropy is inherited from the
reference rather than earned. That is the opposite failure from the unanchored
arm's collapse, and it is why $U$ rather than entropy is the right summary: both
extremes look good on exactly one of the two component metrics.
